Architect Scenario Discussions
Scenario 1: Monolith to Microservices
	Your company has a 10-year-old monolithic application.
	• How would you decide whether migration to microservices is justified?
	• What migration strategy would you choose?
	• How would you minimize business disruption?

Scenario 2: Service Boundaries
	The Order and Payment services frequently change together.
	• Should they remain separate?
	• How would you evaluate whether the current boundaries are appropriate?

Scenario 3: Communication Choices
	An Inventory service must notify multiple downstream systems whenever stock levels change.
	• Would you use synchronous APIs or asynchronous messaging?
	• What trade-offs would you consider?

Key Architect Principles
Service Design
	• Design services around business capabilities.
	• Avoid technical-layer decomposition.
	• Favor cohesion within services.
Communication
	• Use synchronous communication when immediate responses are required.
	• Use asynchronous messaging to improve decoupling and resilience.
	• Be aware of the operational complexity introduced by messaging systems.
Data Ownership
	• Each service should own its data.
	• Shared databases create coupling and hinder independent evolution.
	• Temporary compromises should have a clear migration strategy.
Organizational Alignment
	• Architecture should reflect team structures and business domains.
	• Microservices are as much an organizational decision as a technical one.

Architect Scenario Discussions

	• Would you choose RabbitMQ, Kafka, or Azure Service Bus?
	• What trade-offs would influence your decision?

Scenario 3: Cache Design
	Product catalog data is read thousands of times per minute but changes infrequently.
	• Would you introduce caching?
	• How would you invalidate stale data?
	• What monitoring would you implement?

Scenario 4: Security
	Multiple microservices expose APIs consumed by web and mobile applications.
	• Where should authentication occur?
	• How would authorization decisions be enforced consistently?
	• Would you centralize or decentralize security policies?

Key Architect Principles
Messaging
	• Prefer asynchronous communication when decoupling is important.
	• Design consumers to be idempotent.
	• Expect duplicate message delivery.
Reliability
	• Retries should be deliberate and bounded.
	• Dead-letter queues are essential for operational visibility.
	• Distributed systems fail in unexpected ways; plan accordingly.
CQRS and Event Sourcing
	• Introduce these patterns to solve specific problems.
	• Avoid adopting them solely because they are fashionable.
	• Understand the operational complexity they add.
Caching
	• Cache invalidation remains one of the hardest problems in software engineering.
	• Measure performance gains against consistency requirements.
	• Use distributed caches when multiple application instances are involved.
Security
	• Authentication and authorization should be designed early.
	• Avoid duplicating security logic inconsistently across services.
	• Protect service-to-service communication.
Observability
	• Logs explain what happened.
	• Metrics indicate when something is wrong.
	• Traces reveal how requests flow across services.

Key Architect Principles
System Design
	• Begin with requirements, not technology choices. 
	• Quantify scale before proposing solutions. 
	• Design incrementally and validate assumptions. 
Cloud-Native Thinking
	• Assume failures will occur. 
	• Design for elasticity and automation. 
	• Embrace observability as a first-class concern. 
Reliability
	• Eliminate single points of failure. 
	• Define RTO and RPO based on business needs. 
	• Build recovery mechanisms before incidents happen. 
Governance
	• Standards should enable teams rather than restrict them unnecessarily. 
	• Document architectural decisions. 
	• Revisit standards periodically as the organization evolves. 
Leadership
	• Architects guide trade-offs rather than dictate technologies. 
	• Communication is as important as technical expertise. 
	• Success is measured by the effectiveness of the systems and teams you enable.
